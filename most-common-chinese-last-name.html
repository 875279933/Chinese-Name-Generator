Most Common Chinese Last Name

Discover the top 30 most common Chinese surnames with meanings, pronunciation, and cultural significance

Published: May 6, 2026 | Author: Chinese Name Generator Male

Chinese surnames carry thousands of years of history and cultural significance. Unlike Western naming conventions where surnames are diverse, China has a relatively small pool of family names that are shared by hundreds of millions of people. The most common Chinese last name, Wang (王), is held by over 100 million people. Understanding these surnames helps appreciate Chinese heritage and naming traditions. Below are the top 30 most common Chinese last names ranked by frequency.

Rank #1 王
Pinyin Wáng
Common Spelling Wang

Meaning "king" or "monarch," Wang is the most common Chinese last name, representing royalty and nobility. It symbolizes authority, leadership, and dignity.

Rank #2 李
Pinyin Lǐ
Common Spelling Li

Meaning "plum" or "plum tree," Li is associated with the Tang Dynasty imperial family. It represents resilience, as plum blossoms bloom in winter.

Rank #3 张
Pinyin Zhāng
Common Spelling Zhang

Meaning "to stretch" or "to expand," Zhang originates from archery, symbolizing strength, ambition, and the ability to reach far.

Rank #4 刘
Pinyin Liú
Common Spelling Liu

Meaning "to kill" or "destroy" in ancient context, Liu was the surname of the Han Dynasty emperors. It represents power and historical prestige.

Rank #5 陈
Pinyin Chén
Common Spelling Chen

Meaning "to display" or "ancient," Chen is one of the oldest Chinese surnames. It represents tradition, heritage, and ancestral respect.

Rank #6 杨
Pinyin Yáng
Common Spelling Yang

Meaning "poplar tree" or "aspen," Yang symbolizes resilience and flexibility. Like the tree that bends but doesn't break in the wind.

Rank #7 黄
Pinyin Huáng
Common Spelling Huang

Meaning "yellow" or "golden," Huang represents royalty and prosperity. Yellow was the imperial color in ancient China.

Rank #8 赵
Pinyin Zhào
Common Spelling Zhao

Meaning "to hasten" or "quick," Zhao was the surname of the Song Dynasty emperors. It represents speed, efficiency, and noble lineage.

Rank #9 吴
Pinyin Wú
Common Spelling Wu

Meaning "the sky" or "heaven," Wu represents vastness and openness. It symbolizes unlimited potential and broad horizons.

Rank #10 周
Pinyin Zhōu
Common Spelling Zhou

Meaning "circumference" or "cycle," Zhou was the name of the longest-lasting dynasty. It represents completeness and continuity.

Rank #11 徐
Pinyin Xú
Common Spelling Xu

Meaning "slowly" or "gradually," Xu represents patience and steady progress. It symbolizes the virtue of taking things step by step.

Rank #12 孙
Pinyin Sūn
Common Spelling Sun

Meaning "grandson" or "descendant," Sun represents family lineage and continuity. It emphasizes the importance of family heritage.

Rank #13 马
Pinyin Mǎ
Common Spelling Ma

Meaning "horse," Ma symbolizes speed, strength, and perseverance. Horses were vital in ancient Chinese culture and warfare.

Rank #14 朱
Pinyin Zhū
Common Spelling Zhu

Meaning "vermilion" or "red," Zhu was the surname of the Ming Dynasty emperors. It represents prosperity, good fortune, and imperial authority.

Rank #15 胡
Pinyin Hú
Common Spelling Hu

Meaning "barbarian" or "foreigner" in ancient context, Hu now represents cultural diversity and adaptability.

Rank #16 郭
Pinyin Guō
Common Spelling Guo

Meaning "outer city wall" or "enclosure," Guo represents protection and security. It symbolizes safeguarding family and community.

Rank #17 何
Pinyin Hé
Common Spelling He

Meaning "what" or "how," He also means "to carry." It represents responsibility and the burden of carrying family honor.

Rank #18 林
Pinyin Lín
Common Spelling Lin

Meaning "forest" or "woods," Lin represents abundance, growth, and natural harmony. It symbolizes thriving and flourishing.

Rank #19 罗
Pinyin Luó
Common Spelling Luo

Meaning "net" or "to collect," Luo represents gathering and unity. It symbolizes bringing people and resources together.

Rank #20 高
Pinyin Gāo
Common Spelling Gao

Meaning "tall" or "high," Gao represents aspiration and elevation. It symbolizes reaching for greatness and high standards.

Rank #21 郑
Pinyin Zhèng
Common Spelling Zheng

Meaning "proper" or "upright," Zheng was an ancient state name. It represents integrity, righteousness, and moral uprightness.

Rank #22 梁
Pinyin Liáng
Common Spelling Liang

Meaning "bridge" or "beam," Liang represents connection and support. It symbolizes being a pillar that connects and sustains.

Rank #23 谢
Pinyin Xiè
Common Spelling Xie

Meaning "to thank" or "to decline," Xie represents gratitude and humility. It symbolizes appreciation and polite refusal.

Rank #24 宋
Pinyin Sòng
Common Spelling Song

Rank #25 唐
Pinyin Táng
Common Spelling Tang

Meaning "exaggerated" or "dynasty name," Tang represents the glorious Tang Dynasty. It symbolizes prosperity and cultural golden age.

Rank #26 许
Pinyin Xǔ
Common Spelling Xu

Rank #27 韩
Pinyin Hán
Common Spelling Han

Meaning "Korea" or "ancient state," Han represents the Han Dynasty and Chinese cultural identity. It symbolizes ethnic pride.

Rank #28 冯
Pinyin Féng
Common Spelling Feng

Meaning "gallop" or "horse racing," Feng represents speed and vigor. It symbolizes energetic movement and swift progress.

Rank #29 邓
Pinyin Dèng
Common Spelling Deng

Meaning "to ascend" or "ancient state," Deng represents rising and elevation. It symbolizes upward mobility and advancement.

Rank #30 曹
Pinyin Cáo
Common Spelling Cao

Meaning "class" or "group," Cao was prominent during the Three Kingdoms period. It represents organization and leadership.

Understanding Chinese Surnames

These 30 most common Chinese last names represent over 85% of China's population. Unlike Western countries where surnames are highly diverse, Chinese surnames have remained remarkably concentrated throughout history. This concentration reflects the ancient Chinese clan system and the cultural importance placed on family lineage.

When choosing a Chinese name, understanding the surname is crucial as it forms the foundation of the full name. Each surname carries its own history, symbolism, and cultural weight. Whether you are researching your own heritage or creating a name for a character, these most common Chinese last names provide an excellent starting point.
